3. What this establishes, and what it does not
========================================================================
  * [A] and [B] reproduce all 24 observed bits exactly (sum-ulp = 0). [C] does
    not: it is off by 5 / 2 / 1 ulp on illumination at phases 5 / 7 / 2, and by
    1 ulp on the light scale at phases 1 / 2. Iris's customUniforms therefore
    evaluate float32 per operation, not double-then-round.
  * Consequence for T17: moving the moon formulas out of the fragment shader
    and into a CPU expression does not change the numeric domain. highp float
    in GLSL is float32 and evaluates per-op as well, so the CPU value and the
    value the deleted in-shader formula would have produced sit at the same
    scale. There is no precision argument in either direction - this ticket's
    gain is one source of truth plus three fewer divergent formulas, not
    accuracy.
  * Side finding, written down so nobody "fixes" it later: the Iris result is
    NOT symmetric across the waxing/waning mirror pairs. Phase 2 is
    0x3effffff (one ulp below 0.5) while phase 6 is 0x3f000000 exactly; pairs
    1/7 and 3/5 differ by 2 ulp. That is the ordinary consequence of rounding
    the literal 6.2831853 to float32, and the same asymmetry exists in the
    GLSL version. Maximum relative error 6e-8 - visually null. Do not force
    symmetry.

  NOT established here: anything about what the GPU renders, and anything about
  which way the moon limb points in world space. Those judgements are in
  qa/regression-checklist.md row 7b and are in-game only.
